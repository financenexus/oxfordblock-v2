## Platform

web

## Users

Two primary audiences, served with equal weight:

1. **Brazilian brands & institutions** — marketing/brand managers at football clubs, franchises, museums, tourism boards, theme parks, entertainment/IP owners, banks, airlines, automotive, food/beverage, retailers, influencers. Job: evaluate Oxford as a partner to build a custom branded block collection (licensing/collab).
2. **Brazilian retailers & buyers** — toy stores, stationery shops, bookstores, hobby/geek shops, e-commerce and retail chains. Job: evaluate and carry Oxford's regular product lines.

## Product Purpose

Oxford Blocks Brasil is the Brazilian arm of Oxford (Korea), connecting Brazilian brands and retailers to one of Korea's established block-toy manufacturers. The site is a brand presentation that positions Oxford Brasil as a bridge with **three revenue engines**: (A) institutional/corporate projects — company, factory, machine or HQ in blocks; (B) brand collabs/licensing — collections with brand identity; (C) regular Oxford line distribution for retail. Success = qualified project leads and resale/catalog leads.

## Positioning

> A Oxford transforma marca em objeto. Não vendemos brinquedo. Fabricamos a versão física da sua marca.
> Grandes ideias. Construídas bloco a bloco.
> A sua marca já tem fãs. O que falta é algo que eles possam colocar na estante.

Not a toy distributor — a bridge between Brazilian brands/IP and Oxford Korea's proven custom-block capability. The mechanism no neighbor can copy: direct access to Oxford Korea's manufacturing and its decades of corporate/brand/cultural collaboration experience. The buyer is the **marketing director**, not the toy buyer — bigger budget, faster decision cycle, no shelf space needed.

### Evidence constraints (non-negotiable, from project handoff)
- Corporate roots trace to **1961**; block-toy production began **1992**; Oxford brand milestone **1996**. Do NOT use "40+ years of block manufacturing." Use exact dates.
- "One of the largest premium manufacturers in Korea" is DO-NOT-USE until objective size/market-share evidence exists.
- "Safety certified," "premium material," "high precision," and broad compatibility claims are PENDING until exact technical/certification documentation exists — present as brand aspiration, not verified fact.
- Owner-provided collaboration lists (Korean Air, Hyundai, BTS, etc.) are leads, not independently verified. Present as Oxford's collaboration portfolio per owner/official-archive reference, not as independently audited.
- Never invent market values, percentages, costs, store counts, or forecasts.

## Product Principles

1. **Three engines, not one.** Institutional leads (fastest), collab (bigger ticket), retail (slowest) — sold separately, never blended.
2. **Proof over hype.** Use exact dates and defensible claims; mark what is pending rather than inventing. Seongnam FC and KBO are the proof that closes — they come early, not last.
3. **Marketing director, not toy buyer.** The buyer signs marketing budget, not shelf space. "Transforma marca em objeto," not "vende brinquedo."
4. **Korea-to-Brazil bridge.** Every section reinforces the connection between Oxford Korea's capability and Brazilian opportunity.
5. **Premium and precise.** The visual world must feel like Korean tech craftsmanship, not a toy catalog.
6. **Action-oriented.** Every section drives toward a partner CTA; the closing ask is "Vamos construir uma ideia juntos."
