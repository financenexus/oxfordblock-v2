# Oxford Blocks Brasil — Marketing Plan

Self-contained plan for marketing Oxford Blocks Brasil. Built from the project's own documents (PRODUCT.md, COMPLETE_PROJECT_DOCUMENTATION.md, llms.txt, site copy). No invented figures anywhere — price, MOQ and lead time are always "após o briefing".

---

## 1. What we're marketing

Oxford Blocks Brasil is the Brazilian arm of Oxford (Korea) — a block-toy manufacturer with its own factory in Korea (founded 1961, blocks since 1992, Oxford brand 1996). It is **not a toy distributor**: it sells custom branded block collections to Brazilian brands and institutions, plus the regular Oxford line to retail.

**The buyer is the marketing director, not the toy buyer** — marketing budget, faster decisions, no shelf space needed.

Tagline: *"A Oxford transforma marcas e ideias que ganham vida."*

### Three revenue engines — sold separately, never blended

| Engine | What it is | Cycle | Priority |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A — Institutional/corporate** | Company, factory, machine, HQ or product rebuilt as an exclusive block model (gifts, fairs, campaigns) | Fastest | **Spearhead** |
| **B — Brand collabs/licensing** | Exclusive collections with clubs, franchises, characters, IP owners | Bigger ticket, longer | Parallel pipeline |
| **C — Regular line retail** | Oxford catalog resale to stores | Slowest, capital-intensive | Later |

### Proof that closes (use early, not last)

- **Seongnam FC** — the stadium in blocks + 7 players as individual figures with names and numbers
- **KBO** — player figures, clubhouses and buses for all 10 clubs: a full league program, not a pilot
- **Gwangju FC / Suwon Bluewings** — official club buses in blocks

### Audience

- **Primary:** marketing/brand managers at football clubs, franchises, museums, tourism boards, theme parks, entertainment/IP owners, banks, airlines, automotive, food/beverage, big retailers, influencers
- **Secondary:** retail buyers — toy stores, stationery, bookstores, hobby/geek shops, e-commerce, retail chains

### Channels on hand

- Site (PT-BR, cinematic): preview live, **production domain `oxfordblocosbrasil.com.br` not live yet**
- Instagram `@oxfordblock_official` · WhatsApp +55 11 97462-0848 · `parceria@oxfordblocosbrasil.com.br`
- Partner form with 5 CTAs: projeto institucional · collab de marca · revender Oxford · solicitar catálogo · agendar reunião

## 2. Objective

Generate **qualified partner leads** — measured by partner-form submissions (segmented by type), WhatsApp conversations, meetings booked and proposals sent. The site converts; marketing's job is bringing the right marketing directors to it.

---

## 3. Phase 0 — before any traffic (prerequisite)

Do not spend effort driving traffic to the preview URL: the partner form doesn't work on static hosting, and no lead should land there.

1. Finish go-live: `server.js` running (not static files), `oxfordblocosbrasil.com.br` + HTTPS, Supabase submissions, env vars, LGPD placeholders filled
2. Verify live: submit the real form end-to-end, security headers present, no CSP violations
3. **Until then:** WhatsApp and Instagram DM are the lead channels — say so in outreach

---

## 4. Plays per engine

### Engine A — institutional/corporate (the spearhead)

- **Named-account outreach.** Build a list of 20–30 targets: banks, airlines, automotive, food/beverage, big retailers, tourism boards, museums — anyone who buys corporate gifts, fair activations or campaign objects. Reach marketing/brand managers via LinkedIn + email + WhatsApp, one-to-one, referencing the relevant proof case.
- **Message:** "Sua marca em blocos" — your HQ, product or machine as an exclusive Oxford model. A physical object people keep — this kills "já damos brinde" (a brinde is disposable; a custom block model is not).
- **Lead with the stadium:** "Um clube coreano confiou seu estádio à Oxford. Imagine o seu."

### Engine B — collab/licensing (parallel, longer cycle)

- **Clubs first** — Seongnam FC is the direct analog. Message: "o estádio, o ônibus, os jogadores — a torcida monta." An official collection is a new revenue line *and* a brand object.
- Then franchises, entertainment/IP owners, influencers with product ambitions.
- Open conversations early; don't expect fast closes. Engine A pays while B matures.

### Engine C — retail (light touch)

- Keep the revenda CTA alive and answer it well — zero budget in phase 1. Brazilian proof cases will sell shelf space later better than Korean ones.

## 5. Channel plan

1. **Direct outreach** — primary for A and B. Considered B2B purchase; ads alone don't carry it.
2. **Instagram `@oxfordblock_official`** — the proof engine. Pillars: build-in-progress reels ("bloco a bloco" — the process IS the product demo), Korean proof cases, factory behind-the-scenes, "what if" renders of Brazilian targets. Every post ends on a partner CTA or WhatsApp.
3. **The site is the pitch deck** — its job is making a marketing director feel the premium. Every section must land on a CTA.
4. **LinkedIn** — where marketing directors live. Founder-led posts + "what if your stadium/headquarters in blocks" concept visuals. No paid spend needed at this stage.
5. **Fairs (when budget allows)** — Licensing Expo Brasil and promotional-gift/marketing fairs. A physical prototype in hand beats any ad.

## 6. Content and proof pipeline

- **First asset:** one flagship case-study piece on Seongnam FC + KBO. *Caveat: photos pending verification from Oxford Korea — present per owner/official-archive reference, never as audited fact.*
- **"What if" renders:** mock 3D concepts of famous Brazilian targets (a club stadium, a bank HQ, a branded product). Highest-leverage outreach content — shows the outcome before any meeting.
- **Process content:** conversa → projeto 3D → protótipo → produção na fábrica → entrega. Each step is a post; factory footage answers "é cópia de LEGO?" better than any copy.
- **FAQ content:** the site's real questions are ready-made topics — "A Oxford é uma fabricante?", "Posso transformar meu produto em bloco?", "Vocês trabalham com licenciamento?", "Quanto custa um projeto?", "Qual o prazo?", "Onde são fabricados?"

---

## 7. Funnel

Outreach / IG / LinkedIn → site (WhatsApp while pre-launch) → one of the 5 CTAs → partner form → briefing conversation → proposal.

Every channel lands on the same five doors. Measure which door each engine uses.

## 8. First 90 days

- **Days 1–30:** go-live done · flagship proof case produced · named-account list built · first 20 outreach touches · IG cadence starts (2–3 posts/week minimum)
- **Days 31–60:** outreach wave 2 (clubs + agencies) · first "what if" renders of Brazilian targets · start booking agendar-reunião meetings · harvest the language real replies use
- **Days 61–90:** double down on what produced replies · first proposals out · decide if collab pipeline justifies a dedicated push · the first closed Brazilian deal becomes the permanent proof asset — write that case study immediately

## 9. Measurement

Weekly, from the submissions table (the form's `type` field already segments): submissions by type · WhatsApp conversations started · meetings booked · proposals sent. No dashboards yet — the table tells the story. Spend money only after you see which channel produces meetings.

---

## 10. Red lines (non-negotiable, from the project's own rules)

- Exact dates: **1961 / 1992 / 1996**. Never "40+ years of block manufacturing"
- Never "one of the largest premium manufacturers in Korea" — pending evidence
- Safety / premium material / precision / compatibility claims are aspirations until certification documents exist
- Collab names (Korean Air, Hyundai, BTS…) are Oxford's portfolio per owner/archive — never presented as independently verified by us
- No invented prices, MOQs, deadlines, market figures or forecasts — price is always "após o briefing"
- PT-BR copy; confident, premium, Korean-tech voice; **"bloco a bloco"**, never "tijolo por tijolo"
- Nothing outbound posts or sends without the owner's approval
